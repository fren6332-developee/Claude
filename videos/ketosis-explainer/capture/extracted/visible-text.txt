Topic: How ketosis works — the physiology of switching your body's fuel from
sugar to fat, and its connection to weight loss, insulin, adipose tissue, and
brain health via lactate and BDNF.

Key facts to teach, in order:
1. Ketosis is a normal, physiologically achievable metabolic state — not a
   fad — triggered when carbohydrate intake is low enough that liver
   glycogen (stored sugar) runs low.
2. With glycogen low, the body shifts to burning stored fat for fuel —
   this is the mechanistic link to weight loss.
3. Insulin is the hormone that signals cells to store fat. Lower carb
   intake means lower insulin. Low insulin permits fat cells (adipose
   tissue) to release stored fat into the bloodstream.
4. As fat is released and used, adipose tissue shrinks.
5. The liver converts released fatty acids into ketone bodies (such as
   beta-hydroxybutyrate) — an alternative fuel the brain and muscles can
   use instead of glucose.
6. Exercise adds another brain-health pathway: working muscles produce
   lactate. Lactate crosses the blood-brain barrier and drives BDNF
   (brain-derived neurotrophic factor) expression, supporting brain-cell
   growth and connectivity. (El Hayek et al. 2019, Journal of Neuroscience,
   DOI 10.1523/JNEUROSCI.1661-18.2019 — lactate-induced hippocampal BDNF via
   SIRT1/PGC-1a/FNDC5 signaling.)

Audience: general public, 8th-grade reading level.
Tone: energetic, confident, athletic/coach-style narration.
Visual style: very basic flat/line-art icons only — no photorealism.
